Five things to raise with the Category Manager

Computed on the full 14 months (Aug 2025 to Sep 2026) and independent of the filters above. Figures are rounded; the dashboard tabs hold the detail.

TACOS halved because spend stayed flat while sales tripled, not because anyone made ads more efficient

Evidence. Aug 2026 GMV is ₹1.74 Cr, +301% on Aug 2025, while total ad spend grew +78%. Sep 2026 is +344% on GMV against +64% on spend. TACOS went from 12.6% and 15.2% (Aug and Sep 2025) to 5.6% in both months this year. Daily spend has almost no link to daily GMV (correlation 0.20), though month totals move together (0.75), so budgets look set monthly rather than steered by demand.

So what. Last season shows the exposure: from Oct 2025 to Feb 2026 GMV fell 80% and spend fell only 63%, taking TACOS to 16.9% in Feb. If demand falls the same way this winter, TACOS returns to mid-teens unless budgets flex down.

Action. Set a TACOS ceiling (say 8%) with a rule that scales daily spend to trailing sales, and review it before the Oct festive ramp.

Caveat. Year-on-year can only be checked for Aug and Sep, because the data starts on 1 Aug 2025. The Aug and Sep 2025 base is also small.

Product-level ad spend ignores what sells: a model with 0.6% of GMV takes 7.6% of Sponsored Products budget

Evidence. Each of the 14 models receives roughly the same Sponsored Products budget (₹1.4L to ₹2.9L over 14 months) whatever its sales. Cube Venato Blue (DG-118) has 0.6% of GMV and 7.6% of SP spend, 13× its fair share, with SP-only TACOS of 30%. Lume Designer BLDC is at 14%. The opposite end: Cube AC Black is 21% of GMV with 5.3% of SP spend, SP-only TACOS 0.6%.

Action. Move SP budget from Venato Blue and Lume toward Cube AC Black, Dorado and the Asteria pair, then watch whether rank and units hold. Pause Venato Blue SP until cost and margin are known.

Caveat. This is Sponsored Products only. It is the one spend type that carries a SKU, and it is just 27% of total spend, so full per-model TACOS cannot be computed from this file.

The three models with no cost rate are where the ad leakage sits, so the margin view is blind exactly where it matters

Evidence. Cube Burgundy, Cube Venato Blue and Auriga 4B have no cost rate. Together they are 9.7% of GMV but 24.3% of Sponsored Products spend. Gross margin on the costed 90% of sales is 43.9% (41% to 46% by model). Against that, Venato Blue's SP spend alone consumes about 30 points of GMV before Display, Brands and DSP.

Action. Get cost rates for these three first. Until then do not call them profitable. Margin figures in this dashboard exclude them and say so.

Amazon buys the same GMV for about a third more spend, and half of that spend is upper-funnel with no sales trail

Evidence. Amazon GMV is ₹6.08 Cr and Flipkart ₹5.97 Cr (51/49). Amazon spent ₹58.2L against Flipkart's ₹43.6L (+33%), so TACOS is 9.6% against 7.3%. Amazon Display and DSP are ₹29.9L, 51% of its spend, while Flipkart runs no DSP at all and still matches Amazon on sales. Click quality is identical across every ad type and both platforms (CTR about 1.1%, CPC ₹17.5 to ₹18.2), so nothing in this data separates a good type from a bad one.

Action. Run a holdout on Amazon DSP and Display (pause for 4 to 6 weeks in a few regions or on a few models) and compare GMV. The Flipkart parity suggests the incremental value is unproven.

Caveat. Parity in GMV does not prove DSP does nothing. The two platforms differ in other ways. It is a reason to test, not a conclusion.

Nothing has ever been discounted, and sales are concentrated in a handful of models

Evidence. Realised price equals MOP on every one of the 5,869 sale rows, on both platforms. ASP moves only through model mix. The Oct 2025 festive month still delivered ₹1.33 Cr, 4.2× Sep 2025, at full price. Three models make 45% of GMV and five make 64%; Cube AC Black alone is 21%.

So what. Either price is genuinely held, which makes promotions an untested lever for the slow models, or the GMV column is MOP × units rather than the realised price. If it is the latter, ASP and GMV are overstated wherever deals ran.

Action. Check with Amazon PI and Flipkart Iris whether GMV is realised or list. Then test a deal on a slow model. Protect Cube AC Black stock and Buy Box before the festive period, since a stock-out there hits a fifth of revenue.

Also noted

Flipkart gives no state for 50.6% of located units and no city at all, so a Flipkart-versus-Amazon geographic comparison is not reliable. The location sheet covers only 69% (Amazon) and 72% (Flipkart) of sales units. Returns and cancellations are zero on every row, so net revenue equals GMV and should not be read as true net.

Data health

Each issue was found in the raw workbook and is handled in the numbers shown. Nothing was dropped silently.

How the data was joined

  • One row per physical product. SKU_Master maps each ASIN to its FSN. Every sheet's SKU ID is resolved to that product, so Amazon and Flipkart lines for the same model add up together. Every ID in every sheet matched; none were left unmapped.
  • Dates. Amazon rows are DD-MM-YYYY and Flipkart rows are MM/DD/YYYY, in every sheet. The separator character and the platform agree on all rows, no date failed to parse, and all dates fall between 1 Aug 2025 and 30 Sep 2026.
  • Names. 39 raw product-name strings collapse to 14 after trimming spaces, closing "60 cm" to "60cm" and repairing the garbled m³/hr (shown as mÃ‚Â³). The master name is used for display.
  • Metrics. ASP = GMV ÷ units. DRR = units ÷ calendar days in the range. TACOS = all ad spend ÷ GMV. Net = GMV minus returns. Gross margin = net revenue minus cost rate × units, on models that have a cost rate only.
  • Comparisons. Previous period is the equal-length window immediately before the range. Year-on-year is the same dates one year earlier. Where that window starts before 1 Aug 2025 the tile says n/a, not zero.
  • TACOS when a product filter is on. Only Sponsored Products carries a SKU. If you filter by category, sub-category or model, TACOS becomes SP spend ÷ GMV and is labelled "SP only". Other spend is never spread across products by guesswork.

Why this tool

The brief allowed a Claude-built dashboard. I used a single self-contained page with the cleaned model embedded because it keeps every join, date rule and metric formula visible in one place, filters recompute from row-level data, and it needs no connectors. The data is small (about 13,000 rows after cleaning), so nothing here needs a warehouse.

Limits. It is a snapshot: it does not refresh. For a live version, load the same cleaned tables into a warehouse and rebuild the same measures in Looker Studio or Power BI. The model is deliberately a star schema (SKU spine, then sales, spend, location, age) so the port is mechanical.
